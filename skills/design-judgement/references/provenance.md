# Provenance and coverage

The source collection is not needed to use this skill. All operational references are bundled here. No reference requires the repository, a particular operating system, a helper executable, or an SDD bundle. The separate sdd-skill is relevant only when an actual SDD operation is part of the authorized task.

## What the citations mean

Dates and access statements in adapted modules describe the original collection's review unless explicitly marked as a fresh check. Supplementary research carries its own access notes. Copying a citation does not mean the full source, underlying studies, or a newer edition was re-reviewed during packaging.

Standards have declared applicability, versions, levels, and exceptions. W3C Understanding documents and APG are informative. GOV.UK and other design-system patterns describe an ecosystem. Practitioner heuristics suggest investigations. Empirical findings retain population, task, and method limits. This skill's hypothetical examples establish no real-world result.

For a decision that depends on current exact requirements, consult the governing source. Routine conceptual use can rely on the local summaries without network access. If access is partial, narrow the claim and preserve that limitation.

## Coverage limits

The inherited collection emphasizes digital products and services, with substantial practitioner guidance from NN/g, W3C, GOV.UK, Microsoft, and Google. It is not exhaustive coverage of expert tools, direct manipulation, aesthetics, data visualization, content governance, longitudinal adoption, participatory and cross-cultural research, pricing, or sector-specific obligations. Supplementary entries address only their stated slices of these gaps.

Structural validation and a small set of behavioral exercises can check packaging and reveal reasoning failures. They do not establish that this skill improves designer outcomes across projects. Stronger evidence would compare the same realistic tasks with and without the skill, assessed by human designers for fidelity, justification, uncertainty, inclusion, and task relevance.
